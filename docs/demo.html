Demonstration

Demo guide

Use one controlled failure to show the full agentic operations loop without hiding the decision and safety boundaries.

Before the demo

  • Start Docker Desktop and confirm sufficient CPU and memory are available.
  • Run docker compose config --quiet.
  • Start with docker compose up -d --build.
  • Confirm the Incident Console, Investigation page, Prometheus, and Grafana are reachable.
  • Confirm the database starts with no active incidents for a clean presentation.
Recommended story

Latency incident from signal to recovery

1. Establish normal state

Show live public activity, the generated Locust profile, healthy detector status, and the distributed payment nodes.

2. Inject a controlled fault

Apply latency or timeout through the supported scenario controls. Explain that the fault is local and synthetic.

3. Confirm detection

Wait for telemetry to confirm the symptom. Avoid presenting a scenario command as an incident until the detector observes impact.

4. Review the investigation

Open the investigation payload, evidence, topology, provider, response, confidence, and proposed action.

5. Explain policy

Show whether the action is automatic, approval required, human only, or denied. Approve only when the demo policy requires it.

6. Verify recovery

Show that PulseGuard measures recovery after the action. Resolution should depend on observed service health, not command success.

7. Close the loop

Review the incident history, action evidence, verification result, resolution, and any support assignment.

8. Reset the scenario

Return the environment to a healthy baseline before the next scenario or screenshot.

What to say

"PulseGuard is not just generating a model answer. It observes the system, bounds the investigation evidence, applies policy to the proposed action, executes only approved automation, and verifies recovery before resolution."

What not to claim

  • Do not describe the proof of concept as production-ready.
  • Do not claim that the AI has unrestricted access or autonomous authority.
  • Do not imply that public Wikimedia content is business traffic.
  • Do not treat synthetic scenario ground truth as investigation evidence.
  • Do not show credentials, private URLs, local usernames, or company-specific data.
